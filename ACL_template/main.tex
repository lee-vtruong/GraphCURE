\title{\textsc{CURE}: Constrained Uncertainty Routing with Explanation-Grounded Experts for Retrieved-Evidence Fact Verification}
\author{Anonymous ACL Submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Fact verifiers often conflate two distinct decisions: which verdict is most likely and whether the retrieved evidence warrants a decisive verdict. We study this distinction in a fixed-corpus, text-evidence MOCHEG protocol. \textsc{CURE} (\textbf{C}onstrained \textbf{U}ncertainty \textbf{R}outing with Explanation-Grounded \textbf{E}xperts) combines a verdict-trained direct expert with a rationale-trained complementary expert. Its \emph{Asymmetric NEI Deferral} rule preserves the direct verdict by default and permits only a high-confidence complementary \textsc{NEI} prediction to defer the output. On frozen validation, this constrained rule improves Macro-F1 from 0.6920 to 0.7112 (+1.92 points; paired-bootstrap $P(\Delta>0)=0.9975$), whereas equal averaging and symmetric confidence routing do not. In checksum-audited P1 test inference with frozen $K=5$ and $\tau=.49$, AND improves Macro-F1 by +0.00977 on raw official test ($n=2{,}442$) and +0.00943 on duplicate-safe strict test ($n=2{,}434$). The gains are descriptive patterns concentrated in \textsc{NEI} cases with retrieved evidence available, while retrieval misses remain a failure mode. A separate validation-selected heterogeneous ensemble reaches 0.5551 Macro-F1 on raw P1, and a disagreement-distilled student offers a lower-latency extension.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Automated fact checking is commonly decomposed into evidence retrieval and claim verification \citep{guo2022survey,thorne2018fever}. Modern systems increasingly retrieve heterogeneous evidence and use large language models, but two assumptions remain fragile: that all retrieved passages should be consumed by the verifier, and that a model trained to predict a verdict is also a reliable detector of \emph{retrieved-evidence sufficiency}. We focus on the latter distinction in a controlled text-evidence setting.

MOCHEG \citep{yao2023mocheg} provides a useful setting because it couples three-way truthfulness labels with textual, visual, and social evidence. We deliberately isolate its text-evidence component: all experts receive the same fixed-corpus retrieved passages, so that effects of supervision and decision policy are not confounded by live-web retrieval or visual modeling. This is therefore a fact-verification study on a multimodal benchmark, rather than a claim that the proposed system itself is multimodal.

Our central observation is that two verifiers have complementary error profiles. A direct verifier is strong when evidence appears decisive but can over-commit under non-decisive retrieved context. A rationale-trained expert is more likely to predict \textsc{NEI}, but is not uniformly more accurate and is not assumed to be a calibrated sufficiency estimator. We turn this complementarity into a constrained policy rather than averaging the experts. The direct expert remains the default; only a high-confidence complementary \textsc{NEI} signal can defer the decision. We call this operator \emph{Asymmetric NEI Deferral} (AND). It is a class-constrained cross-expert decision rule, not Bayesian epistemic uncertainty estimation.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textsc{CURE}, a dual-expert formulation that separates direct verdict prediction from a rationale-trained complementary \textsc{NEI} signal;
\item AND, a class-constrained cross-expert rule that permits only \textsc{NEI} deferral, and controlled ablations against grounded-only, posterior averaging, and symmetric routing alternatives;
\item an analysis showing that the benefit is concentrated in \textsc{NEI} cases with high-quality retrieved evidence, not a universal improvement across labels or retrieval conditions; and
\item a disagreement-distilled student as a secondary, lower-latency deployment extension.
\end{itemize}
We distinguish confirmatory results from development diagnostics. The heterogeneous ensemble and AND answer different questions: the former tests broad probability complementarity, while the latter tests a registered, class-constrained decision policy. Both are evaluated on their respective frozen test protocols without test-time parameter selection.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Fact verification and evidence sufficiency.} FEVER established evidence-supported three-way verification at scale \citep{thorne2018fever}; LIAR studied short political claims \citep{wang2017liar}; and MultiFC broadened evaluation across sources and domains \citep{augenstein2019multifc}. These benchmarks make clear that label prediction and evidence sufficiency are not identical. Surveys consequently treat retrieval, evidence selection, reasoning, and calibration as coupled but distinct stages \citep{guo2022survey}. UKP-Athene illustrates the role of multi-sentence textual entailment in evidence-based verification \citep{hanselowski2019ukpathene}.

\paragraph{Retrieval, selection, and reasoning.} Evidence retrieval can dominate downstream verification \citep{zheng2024rav}. RAV combines retrieval with verification, while RAFTS synthesizes contrastive arguments from retrieved evidence \citep{yue2024rafts}. Complex-claim verification decomposes claims before retrieving web evidence \citep{chen2024complex}. These approaches study different evidence regimes from our P1 protocol: they motivate the separation between fixed-corpus evidence selection and live-web retrieval rather than serving as directly interchangeable leaderboard rows.

\paragraph{MOCHEG and multimodal fact checking.} MOCHEG introduced end-to-end multimodal fact checking and explanation generation \citep{yao2023mocheg}. Recent systems pursue different ways to aggregate textual and visual evidence: LVLM4FV uses large vision-language models with reranked evidence \citep{tahmasebi2024lvlm4fv}; MetaSumPerceiver creates claim-specific multimodal summaries \citep{chen2024metasumperceiver}; HGTMFC models high-order interactions with hypergraphs \citep{pang2025hgtmfc}; MEVER jointly addresses retrieval, verification, and explanations \citep{zhang2026mever}; and M-RAV augments cross-modal evidence alignment for zero-shot verification \citep{luu2027mrav}. AMuFC adaptively determines whether visual evidence is needed \citep{jung2026amufc}. These systems motivate the benchmark comparison, but our P1 method consumes retrieved text only. \textsc{CURE} consequently studies constrained routing between differently trained text verifiers, not multimodal fusion.

\section{Task Definition and Protocols}
Given a claim $c$ and retrieved evidence $E=\{e_j\}_{j=1}^{K}$, predict $y\in\{\textsc{Supported},\textsc{Refuted},\textsc{NEI}\}$. The principal P1 protocol uses MOCHEG's fixed corpus and upstream text retrieval; it is not an open-web evaluation. The raw official test contains 2,442 claims. A strict robustness track removes eight verbatim cross-split duplicates and contains 2,434 claims. We report both tracks separately. Development validation contains 1,456 claims.

All routing thresholds are selected on validation or train-only out-of-fold data. Test labels are never used for selecting a seed, threshold, or policy in the confirmatory result. Open-web experiments in Phase C are not mixed into P1 tables.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Direct and rationale-trained experts}
The direct expert $f_D$ receives a claim $c$ and the top-$K$ reranked passages $E_K$ and predicts a three-way distribution $p_D(y\mid c,E_K)$. It is optimized only with verdict cross-entropy. The rationale-trained expert $f_G$ has the same Qwen3-4B-LoRA backbone and the same inference prompt, but is additionally trained to generate a structured teacher rationale. Thus, it remains a verdict classifier at inference rather than a rationale generator. We refer to it as ``grounded'' only in the provenance sense that its auxiliary targets are schema-validated against retrieved passages; this label does not assert that $p_G(\textsc{NEI})$ is a calibrated measure of evidence sufficiency.

Teacher rationales are generated once for training claims by Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct using the \emph{gold training verdict} and the system-retrieved top-five passages. The teacher is instructed to mark a rationale as ungrounded when those passages cannot justify that training verdict. A valid target contains a grounded flag, verdict, key-passage identifiers, free-text reason, and missing-information field. Generation is deterministic (greedy decoding, maximum 512 new tokens), and schema validation rejects malformed key-passage references and unsupported structured outputs. Explanation loss is applied only to valid, grounded targets. This use of training labels is ordinary supervised learning; no validation or test label, and no gold evidence passage, is included in a validation/test prompt.

For a training example $i$, let $z_i$ be the verdict target and $r_i$ the structured rationale target. The two expert objectives are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{D} &= -\sum_i\log p_D(z_i\mid c_i,E_i),\\
\mathcal{L}_{G} &= \mathcal{L}_{D}+\lambda_{r}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rationale}}(r_i), \qquad \lambda_r=0.25.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The rationale term is token-level cross-entropy over the validated structured target. Both experts begin from the same base model, but the headline direct and grounded ensembles were trained under different optimization recipes (specified below); therefore their contrast establishes complementarity, not a causal attribution of every gain to rationale loss. We additionally report a matched-recipe explanation-supervision control and treat it as a causal ablation rather than conflating it with the headline ensemble comparison. The implementation records teacher outputs, manifests, retrieval signatures, and prompt-format hashes. Gold evidence is used only for retrieval diagnostics, never injected into P1 validation or test prompts.

\subsection{Asymmetric \textsc{NEI} deferral (AND)}
The policy uses the direct expert as an anchor:
\begin{equation}
\resizebox{0.96\columnwidth}{!}{$
 \hat y = \begin{cases}
 \textsc{NEI}, & \arg\max_y p_G(y)=\textsc{NEI}\ \land\ p_G(\textsc{NEI})\ge\tau,\\
 \arg\max_y p_D(y), & \text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}$}
\end{equation}
We use $\tau=0.49$, selected on validation and frozen thereafter for the registered $K=5$ router. This rule is intentionally asymmetric: the rationale-trained expert is not permitted to overwrite a direct Supported or Refuted decision with another decisive class. It can only defer to \textsc{NEI} when its complementary \textsc{NEI} posterior is high. AND is therefore distinct from posterior averaging, symmetric confidence routing, and an unconstrained expert switch. $p_G(\textsc{NEI})$ is a learned class posterior, not a Bayesian estimate of parameter uncertainty or a directly validated sufficiency probability.

\subsection{Disagreement-aware distillation}
B19 is a secondary single-model variant. Let $q_D$ and $q_G$ be frozen teacher distributions generated without the held-out fold. For agreement cases, the distillation target is their average; for disagreement cases, a train-only cross-fitted outcome identifies which teacher target to transfer. The confirmed B19 variant minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{DKD}}=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CE}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{KD}}T^2\mathrm{KL}(q\|p_S).
\end{equation}
Here $q$ is the selected teacher target and $T=2$ is the distillation temperature. A counterfactual sufficiency loss was tested during the Fold-0 mechanism screen but was not retained in the confirmed disagreement-KD variant. B19 is reported as a compute-efficient secondary extension, not as a replacement for AND.

\subsection{Competitive ensemble variant}
The CURE-Ensemble test system is a complementary, validation-selected ensemble: five frozen direct seeds plus the three best grounded seeds ranked on validation. The composition $\{100,87,42\}$ is fixed before the official test is evaluated, and its prediction is the arithmetic mean of member verdict probabilities. We also report an unpruned ten-member ensemble (all five direct plus all five grounded seeds) as a no-subset-selection control. This system is a competitive benchmark variant, not an implementation of AND: it answers whether heterogeneous expert predictions are complementary, whereas AND answers whether a constrained \textsc{NEI} deferral is useful.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Data and preprocessing}
MOCHEG contains 15,601 English claims from PolitiFact and Snopes with textual, visual, and social evidence \citep{yao2023mocheg}. Our P1 experiments use a fixed MOCHEG \emph{text} corpus. Qwen3-Embedding-4B retrieves a top-50 dense candidate pool, and Qwen3-Reranker-4B then scores claim--passage utility with an instruction requiring entity, temporal, quantity, location, and negation compatibility. The registered router consumes the top five reranked passages ($K=5$), truncated to 2,200 evidence characters. No gold evidence is injected at validation or test. The open-web C-series snapshot is intentionally excluded from P1 effectiveness tables because it is a different deployment regime.

\subsection{Models and metrics}
All verifiers use Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 with LoRA adapters. The direct ensemble uses seeds $\{13,21,42,87,100\}$; the grounded ensemble contains the validation-selected seeds $\{100,87,42\}$. The representative B1 runs use LoRA rank 16, LoRA scaling 32, dropout 0.05, learning rate $10^{-4}$, AdamW weight decay 0.01, warm-up ratio 0.05, three epochs, per-device batch size 1, and gradient accumulation 16. The headline explanation-grounded runs use the same LoRA rank/scaling/dropout but batch size 2, accumulation 4, and learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$. Hence their raw ensemble comparison is not a matched-optimization causal ablation. For causal attribution, we separately use the five-fold matched-recipe control reported in Section~\ref{sec:matched-exp}. Exact run configurations are retained in JSON summaries.

We report accuracy, class F1, Macro-F1, expected calibration error (ECE) when available, paired percentile bootstrap intervals, and exact McNemar tests. Bootstrap samples are drawn at the claim level with 10,000 iterations. We call $P(\Delta>0)$ a bootstrap probability of superiority, not a frequentist $p$-value. All test table deltas compare predictions with matched claim IDs.

\subsection{Baselines and comparison discipline}
Baselines include direct-only, grounded-only, equal posterior averaging, symmetric confidence routing, no-asymmetric-NEI routing, disagreement-only routing, max-confidence routing, and oracle routing. Literature scores are reported in a separate protocol-aware table. MOCHEG, HGTMFC, AMuFC, MEVER, M-RAV, and open-web systems do not necessarily share evidence, split, modality, or metric definitions; therefore a row is not treated as a direct leaderboard comparison unless its protocol is explicitly aligned.

\subsection{Promotion gates and split discipline}
Every development intervention is governed by a predefined promotion gate stored with the corresponding run configuration. From B6 onward, the project uses duplicate-family-safe train-only folds, fixed training epoch, compute-matched controls, and fresh confirmation folds. A screen-only gain therefore cannot become an official-test claim. CURE-Ensemble is selected on validation and applied once to test. The post-hoc $\tau=.60$ sensitivity result is reported separately and never used as a confirmatory result. The registered AND condition uses $K=5$ and $\tau=.49$; a later $K$ sweep is descriptive rather than a retrospective change to this condition.

We report only the development evidence needed to assess the proposed mechanism in the main paper. Earlier B2--B17 experiments are retained as a compact appendix ledger. \subsection{Official test results and leakage control}
The frozen CURE-Ensemble reaches Macro-F1 $0.55507$ and accuracy $0.57535$ on raw P1 official test, compared with $0.54531$ and $0.56798$ for the direct five-seed ensemble. The paired bootstrap probability of improvement is $0.9839$ with CI $[+0.00097,+0.01850]$. Its score is higher than AMuFC's reported raw-P1 point estimate (Macro-F1 .5400; accuracy .5460), but this is not a paired comparison and the two systems differ in retrieval, backbone, and modality use. We therefore report it as a protocol-aligned point estimate rather than a claim of statistical superiority or state of the art. CURE-Student disagreement distillation reaches Macro-F1 $0.54940$ with one student seed; it is an efficiency diagnostic, not a multi-seed official-test comparison.

AND now has canonical test artifacts on both tracks. On raw P1, AND obtains .55470 Macro-F1 and .57125 accuracy, compared with its independently inferred direct anchor's .54493 and .56798: $\Delta$MF1=+.00977, with 86 helpful and 78 harmful changes and paired bootstrap $P(\Delta>0)=.9546$. On strict P1, it obtains .55433 versus .54490 ($\Delta$MF1=+.00943; 84/77 helpful/harmful; $P(\Delta>0)=.9485$). The raw result passes the preregistered probability-of-superiority threshold by a narrow margin; the strict result is consistent in point estimate but narrowly below that probability threshold. The percentile intervals include zero on both tracks, and McNemar tests are non-significant, so we treat this as confirmatory but modest evidence rather than a claim of large or unqualified statistical superiority. Both reports record manifest, retrieval, input-prediction, and materialized-output hashes. Because raw and strict ranked evidence differ for some shared IDs, each track is inferred independently from the same frozen checkpoints; no intersection or test-time tuning is used.

\section{Additional Ablations and Negative Results}
\subsection{Sentence-selection branch}
A separately named internal experiment, \emph{B18B-Sel}, distills teacher key-passage attribution into a MiniLM cross-encoder that adaptively selects one to three sentences. It is an evidence-selection ablation, not the dual-expert router. On Fold 0, rationale-distilled selection improves a generic cross-encoder by +.00865 MF1 but remains -.00214 behind the retrieval Top-3 control. It was therefore closed after its predefined screen rather than expanded to more seeds or test evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:selector}). This result prevents us from attributing the grounded-expert effect to sentence compression alone; it does not isolate the causal contribution of teacher capacity versus structured-rationale supervision.

\subsection{Why earlier constraint approaches were not promoted}
Earlier B6--B17 interventions supplied useful diagnostics but did not establish a stable main method. B6-A's raw auxiliary ensemble reached .7080 MF1 on validation, yet its bootstrap probability against the compute-matched direct control was .9354, below the predefined .95 gate. B9 and B13 exhibited strong Fold-0 gains but degraded under fresh-fold confirmation. B16's counterfactual verdict curriculum improved Fold 0 by +.01654 MF1 versus its anchor, but aggregated confirmation was -.00104 versus the matched control. These negative outcomes motivate the CURE design choice: preserve a competent direct decision model and use grounded supervision as a constrained retrieved-evidence signal rather than continuously modifying the anchor's decision boundary.

\subsection{Matched-recipe explanation supervision control}\label{sec:matched-exp}
To separate the rationale objective from training exposure, we also ran a five-fold control in which verdict-only and explanation-supervised models use the same B18 training script and optimizer schedule. The explanation model obtains .65757 aggregate MF1 versus .65762 for the matched verdict-only control ($\Delta=-.00006$; bootstrap $P(\Delta>0)=.4940$). Applying the frozen asymmetric router to this matched explanation expert yields .65898, only +.00135 over the control ($P(\Delta>0)=.7273$). Thus, the available matched control does \emph{not} establish that structured rationale loss alone improves average verdict accuracy. We retain the explanation-grounded branch because it yields a complementary NEI-oriented signal under the registered AND interface, but make no stronger causal claim about the source of that complementarity.

\section{Error Analysis}
The dominant helpful transition is direct Refuted/Supported to grounded \textsc{NEI} on gold \textsc{NEI} claims. Harmful transitions are direct Supported to \textsc{NEI} (28 cases) and direct Refuted to \textsc{NEI} (9 cases). This explains why AND improves \textsc{NEI} while reducing decisive-class F1. The failure atlas also shows that routing under missing or rank-2--5 evidence is less reliable. A future system should estimate retrieval reliability before allowing deferral, but using gold rank or QREL status at inference would be invalid.

\section{Discussion}
The results support a narrow interpretation of the contribution. Rationale-trained and direct experts form a complementary pair, but explanation supervision alone is not consistently beneficial: a matched explanation-loss ablation produced approximately zero aggregate gain. Moreover, a decisive-evidence coverage diagnostic finds that the direct posterior has stronger ranking and calibration than the rationale-trained posterior. The useful object is therefore the asymmetric interface between complementary class posteriors, not a demonstrated sufficiency estimator. This interface also exposes a compute--quality trade-off: AND uses eight sequential expert passes, whereas B19 compresses complementary teacher behavior into one student. A stronger external definition of evidence sufficiency remains necessary before claiming that either posterior is calibrated for that construct.

\section{Limitations and Ethical Considerations}\label{sec:limitations}
MOCHEG is a single benchmark with two principal sources and potentially duplicated claims; we therefore report raw and strict tracks. The fixed-corpus protocol does not measure live-web robustness or establish multimodal effectiveness. The grounded expert inherits teacher errors and pseudo-label noise; moreover, teacher rationales are conditioned on the gold \emph{training} verdict, which is appropriate for training but prevents the rationale channel from being interpreted as independent supervision. Subgroup analyses are descriptive and do not justify post-hoc policy tuning. Although AND now has independently inferred, checksum-audited raw and strict test artifacts, its 95\% paired-bootstrap intervals include zero and its strict-track superiority probability is .9485; stronger multi-dataset confirmation is necessary before broad generalization claims. A direct \textsc{NEI}-threshold baseline, a teacher-capacity ablation, and an external dataset remain necessary before submission. Automated fact checking can amplify source and retrieval bias; predictions should be treated as decision support, not authoritative adjudication.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented \textsc{CURE}, a dual-expert constrained routing framework for retrieved-evidence fact verification. Its AND rule improves frozen-validation Macro-F1 by 1.92 points and improves independently inferred raw/strict P1 test Macro-F1 by .977/.943 points under a frozen $K=5,\tau=.49$ policy. The effect is modest and uncertainty-aware: only raw P1 clears the preregistered .95 bootstrap-probability gate. CURE-Ensemble provides a separate validation-selected benchmark variant, while B19 demonstrates a one-model compression extension. The next research steps are external-dataset confirmation and sharper retrieval-reliability gating, rather than post-hoc policy tuning on P1.
