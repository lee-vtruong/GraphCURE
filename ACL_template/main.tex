\title{\textsc{CURE}: Constrained Uncertainty Routing with Complementary Experts for Retrieved-Evidence Fact Verification}
\author{Anonymous Submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Fact verifiers often conflate two distinct decisions: which verdict is most likely and whether the retrieved evidence warrants a decisive verdict. We study this distinction in a fixed-corpus, text-evidence MOCHEG protocol. \textsc{CURE} (\textbf{C}onstrained \textbf{U}ncertainty \textbf{R}outing with \textbf{C}omplementary \textbf{E}xperts) combines a verdict-trained direct expert with a rationale-trained complementary expert. Its \emph{Asymmetric NEI Deferral} rule preserves the direct verdict by default and permits only a high-confidence complementary \textsc{NEI} prediction to defer the output. On frozen validation, this constrained rule improves Macro-F1 from 0.6920 to 0.7112 (+1.92 points; paired-bootstrap $P(\Delta>0)=0.9975$), whereas equal averaging and symmetric confidence routing do not. With frozen $K=5$ and $\tau=.49$, the raw/strict P1 test point estimates are +0.00977/+0.00943 MF1 (95\% CIs $[-.00128,.02109]$/$[-.00187,.02065]$; $P(\Delta>0)=.9529/.9477$). These are directional rather than statistically resolved test results. A matched teacher-capacity ablation finds higher 3B-than-7B rationale-teacher point estimates, but its intervals also include zero, ruling out a simple teacher-scaling explanation rather than establishing a preferred capacity. Post-hoc diagnostic groups suggest a benefit for some \textsc{NEI} cases and identify retrieval misses as a failure mode. A separate validation-selected heterogeneous ensemble reaches 0.5551 Macro-F1 on raw P1, and a disagreement-distilled student offers a lower-latency extension.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Automated fact checking is commonly decomposed into evidence retrieval and claim verification \citep{guo2022survey,thorne2018fever}. Modern systems increasingly retrieve heterogeneous evidence and use large language models, but two assumptions remain fragile: that all retrieved passages should be consumed by the verifier, and that a model trained to predict a verdict is also a reliable detector of \emph{retrieved-evidence sufficiency}. We focus on the latter distinction in a controlled text-evidence setting.

MOCHEG \citep{yao2023mocheg} provides a useful setting because it couples three-way truthfulness labels with textual, visual, and social evidence. We deliberately isolate its text-evidence component: all experts receive the same fixed-corpus retrieved passages, so that effects of supervision and decision policy are not confounded by live-web retrieval or visual modeling. This is therefore a fact-verification study on a multimodal benchmark, rather than a claim that the proposed system itself is multimodal.

Our central observation is that two verifiers have complementary error profiles. A direct verifier is strong when evidence appears decisive but can over-commit under non-decisive retrieved context. A rationale-trained expert is more likely to predict \textsc{NEI}, but is not uniformly more accurate and is not assumed to be a calibrated sufficiency estimator. We turn this complementarity into a constrained policy rather than averaging the experts. The direct expert remains the default; only a high-confidence complementary \textsc{NEI} signal can defer the decision. We call this operator \emph{Asymmetric NEI Deferral} (AND). It is a class-constrained cross-expert decision rule, not Bayesian epistemic uncertainty estimation.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textsc{CURE}, a dual-expert formulation that combines direct verdict prediction with a rationale-trained complementary \textsc{NEI} signal;
\item AND, a class-constrained cross-expert rule that permits only \textsc{NEI} deferral, and controlled ablations against rationale-trained-only, posterior averaging, and symmetric routing alternatives;
\item post-hoc diagnostic analyses that identify \textsc{NEI} improvements and retrieval-miss failure cases, without using diagnostic labels as routing inputs; and
\item a disagreement-distilled student as a secondary, lower-latency deployment extension.
\end{itemize}
We distinguish frozen-test evaluation from development diagnostics. The heterogeneous ensemble and AND answer different questions: the former tests broad probability complementarity, while the latter tests a fixed, class-constrained decision policy. Both are evaluated on their respective frozen test protocols without test-time parameter selection.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Fact verification and evidence sufficiency.} FEVER established evidence-supported three-way verification at scale \citep{thorne2018fever}; LIAR studied short political claims \citep{wang2017liar}; and MultiFC broadened evaluation across sources and domains \citep{augenstein2019multifc}. SciFact further couples scientific claim labels with evidence rationales \citep{wadden2020scifact}. These benchmarks make clear that label prediction, evidence retrieval, and the availability of a convincing rationale are not identical targets. Surveys consequently treat retrieval, evidence selection, reasoning, and calibration as coupled but distinct stages \citep{guo2022survey}. UKP-Athene illustrates the role of multi-sentence textual entailment in evidence-based verification \citep{hanselowski2019ukpathene}.

\paragraph{Retrieval, selection, and reasoning.} Evidence retrieval can dominate downstream verification \citep{zheng2024rav}; evidence selection itself materially changes end-to-end performance \citep{samarinas2021quin}. RAV combines retrieval with verification, while RAFTS synthesizes contrastive arguments from retrieved evidence \citep{yue2024rafts}. Complex-claim verification decomposes claims before retrieving web evidence \citep{chen2024complex}. These approaches study different evidence regimes from our P1 protocol: they motivate the separation between fixed-corpus evidence selection and live-web retrieval rather than serving as directly interchangeable leaderboard rows.

\paragraph{Rationales, selective prediction, and expert routing.} Extractive and generated rationales have been studied as both explanations and learning signals \citep{lei2016rationalizing,atanasova2020factexplanations}; ERASER emphasizes that plausibility and faithfulness require separate evaluation \citep{deyoung2020eraser}. Our matched-recipe control follows this caution: rationale supervision is not assumed to cause a verdict gain. AND is also related to selective prediction with a reject option \citep{geifman2019selectivenet}, but it does not learn a reject head or optimize risk--coverage. Instead, it compares a frozen direct posterior with a complementary expert and permits one class-specific deferral. This differs from sparsely gated mixture-of-experts routing \citep{shazeer2017moe}: CURE evaluates all members sequentially and uses a fixed, interpretable decision rule rather than learned conditional computation. Because softmax confidence need not be calibrated \citep{guo2017calibration}, we report calibration diagnostics and explicitly compare against direct self-deferral.

\paragraph{MOCHEG and multimodal fact checking.} MOCHEG introduced end-to-end multimodal fact checking and explanation generation \citep{yao2023mocheg}. Recent systems pursue different ways to aggregate textual and visual evidence: LVLM4FV uses large vision-language models with reranked evidence \citep{tahmasebi2024lvlm4fv}; MetaSumPerceiver creates claim-specific multimodal summaries \citep{chen2024metasumperceiver}; HGTMFC models high-order interactions with hypergraphs \citep{pang2025hgtmfc}; MEVER jointly addresses retrieval, verification, and explanations \citep{zhang2026mever}; and M-RAV augments cross-modal evidence alignment for zero-shot verification \citep{luu2027mrav}. AMuFC adaptively determines whether visual evidence is needed \citep{jung2026amufc}. These systems motivate the benchmark comparison, but our P1 method consumes retrieved text only. \textsc{CURE} consequently studies constrained routing between differently trained text verifiers, not multimodal fusion.

\section{Task Definition and Protocols}
Given a claim $c$ and retrieved evidence $E=\{e_j\}_{j=1}^{K}$, predict $y\in\{\textsc{Supported},\textsc{Refuted},\textsc{NEI}\}$. The principal P1 protocol uses MOCHEG's fixed corpus and upstream text retrieval; it is not an open-web evaluation. The raw official test contains 2,442 claims. A strict robustness track removes eight verbatim cross-split duplicates and contains 2,434 claims. We report both tracks separately. Development validation contains 1,456 claims.

All routing thresholds are selected on validation or train-only out-of-fold data. Test labels are never used for selecting a seed, threshold, or policy in frozen-test evaluation. Open-web experiments are not mixed into P1 tables.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Direct and rationale-trained experts}
The direct expert $f_D$ receives a claim $c$ and the top-$K$ reranked passages $E_K$ and predicts a three-way distribution $p_D(y\mid c,E_K)$. It is optimized only with verdict cross-entropy. The rationale-trained expert $f_G$ has the same Qwen3-4B-LoRA backbone and the same inference prompt, but is additionally trained to generate a structured teacher rationale. Thus, it remains a verdict classifier at inference rather than a rationale generator. Its auxiliary targets are schema-validated against retrieved passages; this provenance check does not assert that $p_G(\textsc{NEI})$ is a calibrated measure of evidence sufficiency.

The primary rationale targets are generated once for training claims by Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct using the \emph{gold training verdict} and the system-retrieved top-five passages. The teacher is instructed to flag a rationale when those passages cannot justify that training verdict. A valid target contains a retrieval-validation flag, verdict, key-passage identifiers, free-text reason, and missing-information field. Generation is deterministic (greedy decoding, maximum 512 new tokens), and schema validation rejects malformed key-passage references and unsupported structured outputs. Explanation loss is applied only to valid, retrieval-supported targets. A later matched capacity ablation replaces only this teacher with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct. This use of training labels is ordinary supervised learning; no validation or test label, and no gold evidence passage, is included in a validation/test prompt.

For a training example $i$, let $z_i$ be the verdict target and $r_i$ the structured rationale target. The two expert objectives are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{D} &= -\sum_i\log p_D(z_i\mid c_i,E_i),\\
\mathcal{L}_{G} &= \mathcal{L}_{D}+\lambda_{r}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rationale}}(r_i), \qquad \lambda_r=0.25.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The rationale term is token-level cross-entropy over the validated structured target. Both experts begin from the same base model. We report two recipe controls: a five-fold train-only OOF explanation-supervision contrast under a common recipe, and a fresh direct-only control matching the headline rationale-trained data, optimizer, batch size, accumulation, learning rate, epochs, and seeds. The latter reduces the headline recipe confound, although it remains a validation development comparison because both arms select their checkpoints on validation. The implementation records teacher outputs, manifests, retrieval signatures, and prompt-format hashes. Gold evidence is used only for retrieval diagnostics, never injected into P1 validation or test prompts.

\subsection{Asymmetric \textsc{NEI} deferral (AND)}
The policy uses the direct expert as an anchor:
\begin{equation}
\resizebox{0.96\columnwidth}{!}{$
 \hat y = \begin{cases}
 \textsc{NEI}, & \arg\max_y p_G(y)=\textsc{NEI}\ \land\ p_G(\textsc{NEI})\ge\tau,\\
 \arg\max_y p_D(y), & \text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}$}
\end{equation}
We use $\tau=0.49$, selected on validation and frozen thereafter for the registered $K=5$ router. This rule is intentionally asymmetric: the rationale-trained expert is not permitted to overwrite a direct Supported or Refuted decision with another decisive class. It can only defer to \textsc{NEI} when its complementary \textsc{NEI} posterior is high. AND is therefore distinct from posterior averaging, symmetric confidence routing, and an unconstrained expert switch. $p_G(\textsc{NEI})$ is a learned class posterior, not a Bayesian estimate of parameter uncertainty or a directly validated sufficiency probability.

\subsection{Disagreement-aware distillation}
\textsc{CURE}-Student is a secondary single-model variant. Let $q_D$ and $q_G$ be frozen teacher distributions generated without the held-out fold. For agreement cases, the distillation target is their average; for disagreement cases, a train-only cross-fitted outcome identifies which teacher target to transfer. The confirmed variant minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{DKD}}=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CE}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{KD}}T^2\mathrm{KL}(q\|p_S).
\end{equation}
Here $q$ is the selected teacher target and $T=2$ is the distillation temperature. A counterfactual sufficiency loss was tested during the Fold-0 mechanism screen but was not retained in the confirmed disagreement-KD variant. \textsc{CURE}-Student is reported as a compute-efficient secondary extension, not as a replacement for AND.

\subsection{Competitive ensemble variant}
The CURE-Ensemble test system is a complementary, validation-selected ensemble: five frozen direct seeds plus the three best rationale-trained seeds ranked on validation. The composition $\{100,87,42\}$ is fixed before the official test is evaluated, and its prediction is the arithmetic mean of member verdict probabilities. We also report an unpruned ten-member ensemble (all five direct plus all five rationale-trained seeds) as a no-subset-selection control. This system is a competitive benchmark variant, not an implementation of AND: it answers whether heterogeneous expert predictions are complementary, whereas AND answers whether a constrained \textsc{NEI} deferral is useful. We do not have an eight-seed direct-only ensemble control; separating expert heterogeneity from ensemble size would require three additional independently trained direct seeds.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Data and preprocessing}
MOCHEG contains 15,601 English claims from PolitiFact and Snopes with textual, visual, and social evidence \citep{yao2023mocheg}. Our P1 experiments use a fixed MOCHEG \emph{text} corpus. Qwen3-Embedding-4B retrieves a top-50 dense candidate pool, and Qwen3-Reranker-4B then scores claim--passage utility with an instruction requiring entity, temporal, quantity, location, and negation compatibility. The registered router consumes the top five reranked passages ($K=5$), truncated to 2,200 evidence characters. No gold evidence is injected at validation or test. The open-web C-series snapshot is intentionally excluded from P1 effectiveness tables because it is a different deployment regime.

\subsection{Models and metrics}
All verifiers use Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 with LoRA adapters. The direct ensemble uses seeds $\{13,21,42,87,100\}$; the rationale-trained ensemble contains the validation-selected seeds $\{100,87,42\}$. The representative direct-baseline runs use LoRA rank 16, LoRA scaling 32, dropout 0.05, learning rate $10^{-4}$, AdamW weight decay 0.01, warm-up ratio 0.05, three epochs, per-device batch size 1, and gradient accumulation 16. The headline rationale-trained runs use the same LoRA rank/scaling/dropout but batch size 2, accumulation 4, and learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$. The fresh headline-recipe direct-only control uses the latter recipe with the same three seeds and disables only the rationale objective. Exact run configurations are retained in JSON summaries.

We report accuracy, class F1, Macro-F1, expected calibration error (ECE) when available, paired percentile bootstrap intervals, and exact McNemar tests. Bootstrap samples are drawn at the claim level with 10,000 iterations. We call $P(\Delta>0)$ a bootstrap probability of superiority, not a frequentist $p$-value. All test table deltas compare predictions with matched claim IDs.

\subsection{Baselines and comparison discipline}
Baselines include direct-only, rationale-trained-only, equal posterior averaging, symmetric confidence routing, no-asymmetric-NEI routing, disagreement-only routing, and max-confidence routing. Oracle-routing diagnostics are omitted because they do not constitute an implementable baseline. Literature scores are reported in a separate protocol-aware table. MOCHEG, HGTMFC, AMuFC, MEVER, M-RAV, and open-web systems do not necessarily share evidence, split, modality, or metric definitions; therefore a row is not treated as a direct leaderboard comparison unless its protocol is explicitly aligned.

\subsection{Promotion gates and split discipline}
We report a consistent directional-bootstrap reference threshold of $.95$, together with percentile intervals and exact McNemar tests; it is a reporting criterion rather than a claim of preregistration in this anonymized manuscript. The later duplicate-family-safe development studies use train-only folds, fixed training epochs, compute-matched controls, and fresh confirmation folds. A screen-only gain therefore cannot become an official-test claim. CURE-Ensemble is selected on validation and applied once to test. The post-hoc $\tau=.60$ sensitivity result is reported separately and never used as a frozen-test result. The fixed AND condition uses $K=5$ and $\tau=.49$; a later $K$ sweep is descriptive rather than a retrospective change to this condition.

\paragraph{Multiplicity and inferential scope.}
The paper reports multiple related development comparisons, controls, and test-track contrasts. We do \emph{not} apply Bonferroni, Holm, or FDR correction because $P(\Delta>0)$ is a directional paired-bootstrap estimate rather than a frequentist $p$-value, and the comparisons do not form a prospectively specified family of independent hypothesis tests. This does not eliminate the risk of selection across many experiments. Accordingly, no individual bootstrap probability---including values near or above .95---is presented as familywise statistical significance, and all such values are accompanied by percentile intervals and, where available, exact McNemar tests. The paper's claims are limited to frozen-policy point estimates and directional replication across raw/strict tracks; causal and superiority claims remain explicitly unresolved.

\section{Main Results}
\subsection{Mechanism-focused evidence}
We report only the development evidence needed to assess the proposed mechanism in the main paper. Earlier interventions are retained as a compact appendix ledger. They establish four negative findings: evidence compression did not reliably replace document context; symmetric auxiliary objectives and PCGrad did not generalize; calibration and unconstrained routing alternatives were unstable; and counterfactual curricula failed matched-control confirmation. These failures motivate retaining a competent direct verifier and treating the rationale-trained branch as a constrained \textsc{NEI} signal rather than a universally superior classifier.

\paragraph{Reviewer controls.}
We additionally compare AND with a direct-only self-deferral baseline. The latter outputs \textsc{NEI} when $p_D(\textsc{NEI})\geq .23$; its threshold is selected on official validation and frozen before either test track is read. Table~\ref{tab:reviewer-controls} shows that AND exceeds this simpler uncertainty policy by .500 MF1 points on raw P1 and .523 points on strict P1. The directional advantage is replicated on both tracks, but its paired bootstrap probabilities (.7805/.7920) and intervals are not sufficient for a conventional significance claim. Thus, this control rules out a direct \textsc{NEI} threshold as a complete account of the observed AND point estimate, while leaving the magnitude of the incremental benefit uncertain.

\subsection{Official test results and leakage control}\label{sec:canonical-test}
\subsubsection{Configuration provenance and frozen test results}
Table~\ref{tab:configuration-map} is the single configuration map for all raw-P1 scores that might otherwise be read as interchangeable direct baselines. It shows that the four direct-family values differ in seed count, recipe, inference artifact, or post-processing; the headline recipe is not a neutral replacement for the legacy direct recipe. On validation, its direct-only control is .00492 MF1 below the base-recipe direct A0 (.68712 vs. .69205); on raw test, its three-seed output is .01057 below the legacy five-seed value. This pattern is consistent with optimization sensitivity or altered class behavior under the higher-learning-rate recipe, and is an alternative explanation for some rationale-branch behavior. The matched-recipe comparison therefore isolates $\lambda_r$ \emph{within} that recipe but cannot establish that the headline recipe itself is harmless. Likewise, the 7B three-seed average (.53986) is lower than its best individual member (seed 100, .55128), so it should not be interpreted as a monotonic ensemble improvement.

The frozen CURE-Ensemble reaches Macro-F1 $0.55507$ and accuracy $0.57535$ on raw P1 official test, compared with $0.54531$ and $0.56798$ for the previously materialized direct five-seed ensemble. The unadjusted paired bootstrap probability of improvement is $0.9839$ with CI $[+0.00097,+0.01850]$; following the multiplicity policy above, this is directional evidence rather than a familywise significance claim. Its score is higher than AMuFC's reported raw-P1 point estimate (Macro-F1 .5400; accuracy .5460), but this is not a paired comparison and the two systems differ in retrieval, backbone, modality use, and member count. We therefore report it as a protocol-aligned point estimate rather than a claim of statistical superiority or state of the art. CURE-Student disagreement distillation reaches Macro-F1 $0.54940$ for a post-hoc reported single seed (87); it is an exploratory efficiency diagnostic, not a multi-seed or locked-test effectiveness comparison.

AND has canonical test artifacts on both tracks. On raw P1, AND obtains .55470 Macro-F1 and .57125 accuracy, compared with its independently inferred direct anchor's .54493 and .56798: $\Delta$MF1=+.00977, with 86 helpful and 78 harmful changes and paired bootstrap $P(\Delta>0)=.9529$. On strict P1, it obtains .55433 versus .54490 ($\Delta$MF1=+.00943; 84/77 helpful/harmful; $P(\Delta>0)=.9477$). The direct-only self-deferral control is weaker on both tracks (.54970 raw and .54911 strict); AND improves on it by +.00500/+.00523 MF1, respectively. The associated probabilities (.7805/.7920) and all test-track percentile intervals include zero, and McNemar tests are non-significant. We therefore treat the replicated direction as modest directional test evidence, not as statistically resolved confirmation.

\subsubsection{Artifact alignment}
The .00039 raw-MF1 difference between the legacy direct ensemble (.54531) and the independently inferred AND anchor (.54493) arises because the latter is a new canonical raw-manifest inference artifact built to align exactly with the AND members. It is not used in a cross-artifact paired comparison: every AND comparison uses only predictions materialized together on the same manifest. Because raw and strict ranked evidence differ for some shared IDs, each track is inferred independently from the same frozen checkpoints; no intersection or test-time tuning is used.

\subsubsection{Validation--test gap}
The larger validation--test difference is not interpreted as a model improvement. Validation was used for seed and threshold development and has a distinct claim composition. The contraction from +.01916 MF1 on validation to +.00977/+.00943 on raw/strict test is consistent with development-selection sensitivity, but this study cannot apportion it among distribution shift, optimization, and selection.

\subsubsection{Routing versus averaging}
Raw CURE-Ensemble (.55507) is marginally above raw AND (.55470), although A2 averaging is unhelpful on validation. This difference does not establish that either decision rule dominates: the systems have distinct validation-frozen compositions and objectives, and neither is an ablation of the other. Both reports record manifest, retrieval, input-prediction, and materialized-output hashes.

\subsection{Why earlier constraint approaches were not promoted}
Earlier interventions supplied useful diagnostics but did not establish a stable main method. A source-aware auxiliary ensemble reached .7080 MF1 on validation, yet its bootstrap probability against the compute-matched direct control was .9354, below the reported .95 reference threshold. Calibration and curriculum variants exhibited strong Fold-0 gains but degraded under fresh-fold confirmation. A counterfactual verdict curriculum improved Fold 0 by +.01654 MF1 versus its anchor, but aggregated confirmation was -.00104 versus the matched control. These negative outcomes motivate the CURE design choice: preserve a competent direct decision model and use a rationale-trained complementary signal through a constrained interface rather than continuously modifying the anchor's decision boundary.

\subsection{Matched-recipe explanation supervision control}\label{sec:matched-exp}
This is a distinct control from the headline-recipe contrast in Table~\ref{tab:headline-control}. We ran a five-fold train-only OOF experiment in which verdict-only and explanation-supervised models use one common training script and optimizer schedule. The explanation model obtains .65757 aggregate MF1 versus .65762 for the matched verdict-only control ($\Delta=-.00006$; bootstrap $P(\Delta>0)=.4940$). Applying the frozen asymmetric router to this matched explanation expert yields .65898, only +.00135 over the control ($P(\Delta>0)=.7273$). These OOF values are neither validation nor test results and cannot be directly compared with the .71121 validation policy score. They show that structured rationale loss alone does \emph{not} improve average verdict accuracy under that common recipe.

\subsection{Headline-recipe direct-only control}\label{sec:headline-control}

\subsection{Matched teacher-capacity ablation}\label{sec:teacher-capacity}
The capacity alternative is tested directly rather than inferred from the headline result. \section{Error Analysis}
The dominant helpful transition is direct Refuted/Supported to rationale-trained \textsc{NEI} on gold \textsc{NEI} claims. Harmful transitions are direct Supported to \textsc{NEI} (28 cases) and direct Refuted to \textsc{NEI} (9 cases). This explains why AND improves \textsc{NEI} while reducing decisive-class F1. The failure atlas also shows that routing under missing or rank-2--5 evidence is less reliable. A future system should estimate retrieval reliability before allowing deferral, but using gold rank or QREL status at inference would be invalid.

\section{Discussion}
The results support a narrow interpretation of the contribution. Rationale-trained and direct experts form a complementary pair, but explanation supervision alone is not consistently beneficial: a matched explanation-loss ablation produced approximately zero aggregate gain. The headline-recipe control is directionally favorable to rationale training, yet its validation and test intervals include zero. Crucially, this limited contrast is recipe-conditional: $\lambda_r>0$ may transfer structured rationale information, or it may partly regularize adverse behavior induced by the headline optimization schedule. It therefore does not establish a recipe-invariant value of rationale supervision. The matched teacher-capacity control further weakens a capacity-based explanation: replacing the 7B rationale teacher with its 3B counterpart raises the raw/strict point estimates by .844/.753 MF1, but neither comparison resolves statistically. Accordingly, the evidence does not identify a causal mechanism within rationale supervision, and it does not support the claim that a larger teacher is responsible for the branch's behavior.

Moreover, a decisive-evidence coverage diagnostic finds that the direct posterior has stronger ranking and calibration than the rationale-trained posterior (AUROC .6486 vs. .6155; Brier .1122 vs. .1406); this diagnostic uses gold labels only after prediction and is never a router input. AND also exceeds a frozen direct self-deferral baseline on both test tracks, although that incremental comparison is not statistically resolved. The useful object is therefore the asymmetric interface between complementary class posteriors, not a demonstrated sufficiency estimator. This interface also exposes a compute--quality trade-off: AND uses eight sequential expert passes, whereas \textsc{CURE}-Student compresses complementary teacher behavior into one student. A stronger external definition of evidence sufficiency remains necessary before claiming that either posterior is calibrated for that construct.

\section{Limitations and Ethical Considerations}\label{sec:limitations}
MOCHEG is a single benchmark with two principal sources and potentially duplicated claims; we therefore report raw and strict tracks. The fixed-corpus protocol does not measure live-web robustness or establish multimodal effectiveness. The rationale-trained expert inherits teacher errors and pseudo-label noise; moreover, teacher rationales are conditioned on the gold \emph{training} verdict, which is appropriate for training but prevents the rationale channel from being interpreted as independent supervision. Although the matched teacher-capacity control compares 3B and 7B, it evaluates only two capacities from one teacher family and cannot identify why their target distributions differ. The headline-recipe direct control reduces causal ambiguity and preserves a positive point estimate on both test tracks, but its validation and test intervals include zero and its inference is conditional on that optimization recipe. The multiple related development and test comparisons create a real selection/multiplicity risk; we therefore make no familywise significance claim and report all inspected alternatives, including negative results. Subgroup analyses are descriptive and do not justify post-hoc policy tuning. Although AND now has independently inferred raw and strict test artifacts, its 95\% paired-bootstrap intervals include zero and strict-track directional evidence is .9477; stronger multi-dataset confirmation is needed before broad generalization claims. External-dataset evaluation and a broader teacher-family study remain future work. Automated fact checking can amplify source and retrieval bias; predictions should be treated as decision support, not authoritative adjudication.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented \textsc{CURE}, a dual-expert constrained routing framework for retrieved-evidence fact verification. Its AND rule improves frozen-validation Macro-F1 by 1.92 points and has raw/strict P1 test point-estimate gains of .977/.943 points under a fixed $K=5,\tau=.49$ policy. A same-recipe direct-only control retains positive raw/strict rationale-trained differences of .512/.685 MF1 points, but their intervals include zero. A matched 3B-versus-7B teacher-capacity ablation does not support simple teacher scaling as the explanation: 3B has higher raw/strict point estimates, while the paired intervals remain unresolved. Thus the routing, recipe-control, and capacity evidence should be read as directional rather than statistically resolved causal proof. CURE-Ensemble provides a separate validation-selected benchmark variant, while \textsc{CURE}-Student demonstrates a one-model compression extension. The next research steps are external-dataset evaluation, broader teacher-family controls, and sharper retrieval-reliability gating, rather than post-hoc policy tuning on P1.
