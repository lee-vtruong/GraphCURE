\author{Anonymous ACL Submission}

\begin{abstract}
Multimodal fact-checking systems are vulnerable to two coupled errors: retrieved evidence can be incomplete, and a verifier optimized for a verdict can remain overconfident when the evidence does not warrant one. We study this problem in a fixed-corpus MOCHEG protocol, isolating verifier and decision-policy effects from live-web variation. We introduce \textsc{GraphCURE} (\textbf{G}rounded \textbf{C}ross-\textbf{E}xpert \textbf{U}ncertainty \textbf{R}outing), a framework that combines a direct verdict expert with a grounded rationale expert. Its asymmetric epistemic-deferral policy preserves the direct verdict by default and emits \textsc{NEI} only when the grounded expert detects insufficient evidence with frozen high confidence. On frozen validation, the full policy improves Macro-F1 from 0.6920 to 0.7112 (+1.92 points; paired-bootstrap $P(\Delta>0)=0.9975$). We report broad component, top-$K$, threshold, subgroup, efficiency, and negative-ablation analyses. A validation-selected eight-model heterogeneous ensemble reaches 0.5551 Macro-F1 on the raw official P1 test ($n=2{,}442$), exceeding the 0.5400 reported by the directly comparable AMuFC baseline; a one-model disagreement-distilled student reaches 0.5494. We distinguish raw official and deduplicated strict tracks, never use test labels to select confirmatory policies, and identify an unreconciled B18B routing artifact as a pre-submission TODO.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Automated fact checking is commonly decomposed into evidence retrieval and claim verification \citep{guo2022survey,thorne2018fever}. Modern systems increasingly retrieve heterogeneous evidence and use large language models, but two assumptions remain fragile: that all retrieved passages should be consumed by the verifier, and that an expert trained for verdict prediction is also calibrated about evidence sufficiency. These assumptions are particularly consequential for multimodal fact checking, where evidence may be noisy, missing, duplicated, or distributed across modalities.

MOCHEG \citep{yao2023mocheg} provides a useful setting because it couples three-way truthfulness labels with retrieved textual and visual evidence and explanations. Existing MOCHEG systems model cross-modal interactions directly, for example with hypergraph transformers \citep{pang2025hgtmfc}. However, comparisons are difficult when systems use different retrieval assumptions. We therefore first establish a fixed-corpus, text-retrieval protocol and then study the verifier and routing decisions under identical evidence.

Our central observation is that two verifiers have complementary error profiles. A direct verifier is strong when evidence appears decisive but can over-commit under missing evidence. A grounded expert, trained with structured explanations, is more sensitive to insufficiency but is not uniformly better. We turn this complementarity into a selective policy rather than averaging the experts. The direct expert remains the default; only a high-confidence grounded NEI signal can defer the decision.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textsc{GraphCURE}, a reproducible protocol-separated framework in which grounded explanations act as an evidence-sufficiency sensor rather than a symmetric second classifier;
\item an asymmetric cross-expert decision rule that isolates the contribution of \textsc{NEI} deferral from ensembling, confidence routing, and grounded-only prediction;
\item a fully auditable empirical ledger spanning B1--B19, including negative results, train-only OOF controls, frozen validation ablations, raw official test, and strict robustness evaluation; and
\item B19 disagreement-aware knowledge distillation, a compute-efficient single-model alternative that transfers complementary teacher behavior without inference-time routing.
\end{itemize}
We do not claim that every number in the internal development history is directly comparable. Official-test claims are restricted to frozen, auditable artifacts; unresolved discrepancies are marked in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Fact verification and evidence sufficiency.} FEVER established evidence-supported three-way verification at scale \citep{thorne2018fever}; LIAR studied short political claims \citep{wang2017liar}; and MultiFC broadened evaluation across sources and domains \citep{augenstein2019multifc}. These benchmarks make clear that label prediction and evidence sufficiency are not identical. Surveys consequently treat retrieval, evidence selection, reasoning, and calibration as coupled but distinct stages \citep{guo2022survey}. UKP-Athene illustrates the role of multi-sentence textual entailment in evidence-based verification \citep{hanselowski2019ukpathene}.

\paragraph{Retrieval, selection, and reasoning.} Evidence retrieval can dominate downstream verification \citep{zheng2024rav}. RAV combines retrieval with verification, while RAFTS synthesizes contrastive arguments from retrieved evidence \citep{yue2024rafts}. Complex-claim verification decomposes claims before retrieving web evidence \citep{chen2024complex}. These approaches study different evidence regimes from our P1 protocol: they motivate the separation between fixed-corpus evidence selection and live-web retrieval rather than serving as directly interchangeable leaderboard rows.

\paragraph{Multimodal fact checking.} MOCHEG introduced end-to-end multimodal fact checking and explanation generation \citep{yao2023mocheg}. Recent systems pursue different ways to aggregate evidence: LVLM4FV uses large vision-language models with reranked evidence \citep{tahmasebi2024lvlm4fv}; MetaSumPerceiver creates claim-specific multimodal summaries \citep{chen2024metasumperceiver}; HGTMFC models high-order interactions with hypergraphs \citep{pang2025hgtmfc}; MEVER jointly addresses retrieval, verification, and explanations \citep{zhang2026mever}; and M-RAV augments cross-modal evidence alignment for zero-shot verification \citep{luu2027mrav}. AMuFC adaptively determines whether visual evidence is needed \citep{jung2026amufc}, while Entailed Opinion stresses entailment-oriented reasoning \citep{kumar2025entailed}. \textsc{GraphCURE} shares the adaptive-evidence motivation but studies an observable uncertainty-routing problem between two differently trained verifiers under an explicitly controlled P1 evidence protocol.

\section{Task Definition and Protocols}
Given a claim $c$ and retrieved evidence $E=\{e_j\}_{j=1}^{K}$, predict $y\in\{\textsc{Supported},\textsc{Refuted},\textsc{NEI}\}$. The principal P1 protocol uses MOCHEG's fixed corpus and upstream text retrieval; it is not an open-web evaluation. The raw official test contains 2,442 claims. A strict robustness track removes eight verbatim cross-split duplicates and contains 2,434 claims. We report both tracks separately. Development validation contains 1,456 claims.

All routing thresholds are selected on validation or train-only out-of-fold data. Test labels are never used for selecting a seed, threshold, or policy in the confirmatory result. Open-web experiments in Phase C are not mixed into P1 tables.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Direct and grounded experts}
The direct expert $f_D$ receives claim $c$ and top-$K$ retrieved passages $E_K$ and predicts a three-way distribution $p_D(y\mid c,E_K)$. It is trained with ordinary verdict cross-entropy. The grounded expert $f_G$ shares the backbone and input format but is trained with teacher-distilled structured explanations. Each teacher target contains: (i) a verdict, (ii) selected key-evidence identifiers, (iii) sufficiency/polarity fields, and (iv) an explicit missing-information field. Hence the extra supervision makes insufficiency observable in the target representation without requiring the model to generate an explanation at inference.

For a training example $i$, let $z_i$ be the verdict target and $r_i$ the structured rationale target. The two expert objectives are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{D} &= -\sum_i\log p_D(z_i\mid c_i,E_i),\\
\mathcal{L}_{G} &= \mathcal{L}_{D}+\lambda_{r}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rationale}}(r_i).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The project records teacher outputs, manifests, retrieval signatures, and prompt-format hashes. Gold evidence is used only for retrieval diagnostics, never injected into P1 validation or test prompts.

\subsection{Asymmetric epistemic deferral}
The policy uses the direct expert as an anchor:
\begin{equation}
\resizebox{0.96\columnwidth}{!}{$
 \hat y = \begin{cases}
 \textsc{NEI}, & \arg\max_y p_G(y)=\textsc{NEI}\ \land\ p_G(\textsc{NEI})\ge\tau,\\
 \arg\max_y p_D(y), & \text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}$}
\end{equation}
We use $\tau=0.49$, selected on validation and frozen thereafter. This rule is intentionally asymmetric: the grounded expert is not permitted to overwrite a direct Supported or Refuted decision with another decisive class. It can only defer when it detects insufficiency. This preserves the direct verifier's decisive-class calibration while using the grounded branch as an epistemic sensor. It is therefore distinct from posterior averaging, symmetric confidence routing, and the oracle routing upper bound.

\subsection{Disagreement-aware distillation}
B19 is a secondary single-model variant. Let $q_D$ and $q_G$ be frozen teacher distributions. For agreement cases, the distillation target is their average; for disagreement cases, the train-only held-out outcome identifies the teacher target during cross-fitted training. The student minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CE}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{KD}}T^2\mathrm{KL}(q\|p_S)+\lambda_{\mathrm{cf}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{cf}},
\end{equation}
where $q$ is the selected teacher target and $T$ is the distillation temperature. A counterfactual term compares an evidence set with a version in which teacher-identified key passages are omitted. B19 is reported as a compute-efficient secondary contribution, not as a replacement for the main B18B routing analysis.

\subsection{Validation-selected heterogeneous ensemble}
The confirmatory B18A test system is a complementary, validation-selected ensemble: five frozen B1 direct seeds plus the three best B18A grounded seeds ranked on validation. The composition $\{100,87,42\}$ is fixed before the official test is evaluated. Its prediction is the arithmetic mean of member verdict probabilities. We also report an unpruned ten-member ensemble (all five direct plus all five grounded seeds) as a no-subset-selection control. This is important because B18A ensemble selection and B18B routing answer different questions: the former is the principal P1 benchmark system; the latter is the main decision-policy ablation.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Data and preprocessing}
MOCHEG contains 15,601 English claims from PolitiFact and Snopes with textual, visual, and social evidence \citep{yao2023mocheg}. Our P1 experiments use the project manifest, a fixed MOCHEG text corpus, Qwen3 dense retrieval, and reranking. We retain the resulting evidence order and use $K=5$ passages unless an evidence ablation changes $K$. No gold evidence is injected at validation or test. The open-web C-series snapshot is intentionally excluded from P1 effectiveness tables: it tests a different deployment regime.

\subsection{Models and metrics}
All verifiers use Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 with LoRA adapters. The direct anchor uses five seeds $\{13,21,42,87,100\}$; B18A uses grounded-rationale distillation; B18B consumes the frozen direct and grounded predictions. The representative runs use LoRA rank 16, LoRA scaling 32, dropout 0.05, learning rate $10^{-4}$, AdamW weight decay 0.01, warm-up ratio 0.05, three epochs, per-device batch size 1, and gradient accumulation 16. Evidence is truncated to 2,200 characters for the standard grounded prompts; exact run configurations are retained in each JSON summary.

We report accuracy, class F1, Macro-F1, expected calibration error (ECE) when available, paired percentile bootstrap intervals, and exact McNemar tests. Bootstrap samples are drawn at the claim level with 10,000 iterations. We call $P(\Delta>0)$ a bootstrap probability of superiority, not a frequentist $p$-value. All test table deltas compare predictions with matched claim IDs.

\subsection{Baselines and comparison discipline}
Baselines include direct-only, grounded-only, equal posterior averaging, symmetric confidence routing, no-asymmetric-NEI routing, disagreement-only routing, max-confidence routing, and oracle routing. Literature scores are reported in a separate protocol-aware table. MOCHEG, HGTMFC, AMuFC, MEVER, M-RAV, and open-web systems do not necessarily share evidence, split, modality, or metric definitions; therefore a row is not treated as a direct leaderboard comparison unless its protocol is explicitly aligned.

\subsection{Promotion gates and split discipline}
Every development intervention is governed by a predeclared promotion gate. From B6 onward, the project uses duplicate-family-safe train-only folds, fixed training epoch, compute-matched controls, and fresh confirmation folds. A screen-only gain therefore cannot become an official-test claim. The B18A ensemble is selected on validation and applied once to test. The post-hoc $\tau=.60$ sensitivity result is reported separately and never used as the main confirmatory result. This distinction is central to interpreting the long experimental record in Table~\ref{tab:trajectory}.

\subsection{Official test results and leakage control}
The frozen B18A heterogeneous ensemble reaches Macro-F1 $0.55507$ and accuracy $0.57535$ on raw P1 official test, compared with the B1 five-seed baseline of $0.54531$ and $0.56798$. The paired bootstrap probability of improvement is $0.9839$ with CI $[+0.00097,+0.01850]$. Compared with AMuFC's reported raw-P1 point estimate (Macro-F1 .5400; accuracy .5460), this is a +1.507 Macro-F1-point and +2.935 accuracy-point difference. This is a point-estimate comparison only: AMuFC predictions are unavailable, so no paired test can be performed. B19 disagreement distillation reaches Macro-F1 $0.54940$ with one student model. These numbers were produced after validation-only freezing.

The test-peak $\tau=0.60$ result (Macro-F1 $0.56166$) is exploratory and is not used as the main claim because the threshold was identified after inspecting test outcomes. Similarly, any B18B row whose canonical prediction file is not yet reconciled remains TODO.

\section{Additional Ablations and Negative Results}
\subsection{Sentence-selection branch}
The repository contains a separately named B18B sentence-selector study; to avoid ambiguity, we call it \emph{B18B-Sel} in this paper. B18B-Sel uses rationale-distilled cross-encoder scores to adaptively select one to three sentences. It is useful as an evidence-selection ablation, but it is \emph{not} the dual-expert routing method in Figure~\ref{fig:method}. On Fold 0, rationale-distilled selection improves a generic cross-encoder by +.00865 MF1 but is still -.00214 behind the retrieval Top-3 control. Consequently it was closed after the preregistered screen rather than expanded to more seeds or test evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:selector}). This negative result guards against attributing B18A gains to sentence compression alone.

\subsection{Why earlier constraint approaches were not promoted}
Earlier B6--B17 interventions supplied useful diagnostics but did not establish a stable main method. B6-A's raw auxiliary ensemble reached .7080 MF1 on validation, yet its bootstrap probability against the compute-matched direct control was .9354, below the preregistered .95 gate. B9 and B13 exhibited strong Fold-0 gains but degraded under fresh-fold confirmation. B16's counterfactual verdict curriculum improved Fold 0 by +.01654 MF1 versus its anchor, but aggregated confirmation was -.00104 versus the matched control. These negative outcomes motivate the B18 design choice: preserve a competent direct decision model and use grounded supervision as a selective uncertainty signal rather than continuously modifying the anchor's decision boundary.

\section{Efficiency and Robustness Analysis}

B19 is approximately 7.97 times faster than B18B. All ensemble timings are sequential; peak allocated memory is therefore a per-checkpoint peak, not a simultaneous eight-model memory requirement. Subset effectiveness scores produced by this benchmark are diagnostic only.

\section{Error Analysis}
The dominant helpful transition is direct Refuted/Supported to grounded NEI on gold NEI claims. Harmful transitions are direct Supported to NEI (28 cases) and direct Refuted to NEI (9 cases). This asymmetry explains why B18B improves NEI while slightly reducing decisive-class F1. The failure atlas also shows that routing under missing or rank-2--5 evidence is less reliable. A future system should estimate retrieval reliability before allowing deferral, but using gold rank or QREL status at inference would be invalid.

\section{Discussion}
The results support a narrow interpretation of the contribution. Evidence-grounded explanations are useful as a sufficiency sensor, but explanation supervision alone is not consistently beneficial: a matched explanation-loss ablation produced approximately zero aggregate gain. The useful object is the asymmetric interface between epistemic sensing and verdict prediction. This interface also exposes a compute--quality trade-off: B18B uses eight sequential expert passes, whereas B19 compresses the complementarity into one student.

\section{Limitations and Ethical Considerations}\label{sec:limitations}
MOCHEG is a single benchmark with two principal sources and potentially duplicated claims; we therefore report raw and strict tracks. The fixed-corpus protocol does not measure live web robustness. The grounded expert inherits teacher errors and pseudo-label noise. Subgroup analyses are descriptive and do not justify post-hoc policy tuning. The current repository contains inconsistent B18B test summaries; the final paper must replace the TODO row with a checksum-matched canonical evaluation. Automated fact checking can amplify source and retrieval bias; predictions should be treated as decision support, not authoritative adjudication.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented GraphCURE-B18B, a dual-expert asymmetric epistemic-deferral policy for evidence-grounded fact checking. On frozen validation it improves Macro-F1 by 1.92 points with strong paired-bootstrap evidence, and subgroup analysis attributes the gain to correcting NEI decisions when high-quality evidence is available. B19 demonstrates that complementary experts can be compressed into a single efficient student. The remaining priority is canonicalizing the B18B official-test artifact and evaluating frozen policies on an external dataset before making a broad cross-domain claim.
